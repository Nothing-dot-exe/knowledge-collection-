## Thermodynamic Concentration Inequalities: Controlling Uncertainty in Finite-Time and Small-Sample Thermodynamic Inference

Rick Bebon ∗ and Aljaˇ z Godec †

Mathematical Physics and Stochastic Dynamics, Faculty of Mathematics and Physics, University of Freiburg

We derive nonasymptotic upper bounds on the probability that a generalized current of a geometrically ergodic diffusion observed for any amount of time, or its sample mean over any arbitrary sample size, deviates from the stationary mean by more than any given amount. The concentrationof-measure behavior of generalized currents is universally governed by the relaxation time of the underlying dynamics, the locally observed dissipation rate, and the intrinsic local fluctuations of the observable. We uncover stark qualitative and quantitative differences in fluctuations in and out of thermodynamic equilibrium. We further obtain refined inverse thermodynamic uncertainty relations, bounding the variance of generalized currents from above. We construct nonasymptotic confidence intervals for controlling uncertainty in thermodynamic inference from small data, i.e., from short trajectories and small samples, and provide the first quantitative answer to when a trajectory is sufficiently long and a sample is sufficiently large. As an illustration, we apply our results to currents observed on a two-dimensional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process in and out of equilibrium and show how they can be used to rigorously detect broken detailed balance.

Additive functionals of stochastic sample paths-often referred to as time-averaged observables [1-4, 6]-are central to the analysis of stochastic time series [7-9], where time-averaging along a single or a handful of sufficiently long trajectories serves as a proxy for the traditional ensemble average. On the theoretical side, Stratonovichtype functionals J t ≡ t - 1 ∫ t 0 U ( X s ) ◦ dX s , so-called generalized currents, have moved to the center of nonequilibrium statistical mechanics and stochastic thermodynamics, where they define energy-like quantities of mesoscopic systems along individual trajectories [10-13], and extend the continuity equation for additive path functionals [3, 4]. Finite-time fluctuations of J t obey fluctuation theorems [11, 14, 15], and their tails encode rare events of thermodynamic significance, e.g., apparent 'violations' of the second law [14, 16]. Fluctuations are further constrained by the thermodynamic uncertainty relation (TUR) [17-20] which imposes a noise floor on any current estimate.

Reliable inference of the stationary mean E µ [ J t ] from a time average or, more generally, from the sample mean ̂ J n,t ≡ n - 1 ∑ n i =1 J ( i ) t over n statistically independent paths of length t hinges on a trajectory being 'sufficiently long' and a sample 'sufficiently large'. What is 'sufficient' is far from trivial a priori and subsampling poses an additional challenge [52]. Central-limit and bootstrap methods may underestimate the uncertainty and fail to guarantee coverage of the confidence level [53-56], while Bayesian approaches (contrary to common perception [5760]) do not evade the small-sample problem, as they remain sensitive to, and biased by, the prior choice [61]. Fluctuations of J t are therefore intrinsically interesting and crucial for interpreting single-molecule and particletracking measurements [21-33], as well as for controlling uncertainty in thermodynamic inference [19, 20, 34, 35]. Specifically, TUR-based inference hinges on sampling the second and higher-order moments [36-40] of the current functional, and inference of free-energy differences from nonequilibrium work measurements via the Jarzynski [41] or Crooks [42] relations requires sufficient sampling of work distribution tails [43-51].

̂ J n,t remains elusive. Required but missing is nonasymptotic control of the full tails of J t at finite t and of ̂ J n,t at finite n . Indeed, the large deviation description of current fluctuations [62-66] is asymptotic in t and requires solving a nontrivial spectral problem for a non-self-adjoint tilted generator [64, 66], which is tractable only for simple models [67-69] and otherwise necessitates numerical or sampling methods [7072]. Exact finite-time results are limited to variances and correlations [3, 4], and the TUR and its inverse (iTUR) [73, 74] constrain only the second moment. Concentration inequalities provide nonasymptotic tail control but existing literature is limited to density-type functionals [75-80] or, for currents, to Markov jump processes [73]. A concentration analysis of Stratonovich-type functionals of continuous state-space dynamics remains, to our knowledge, unexplored.

At detailed balance ( j s = 0), Σ U = Σ U ∞ = 0 and the stationary mean of any generalized current vanishes identically [107], yet fluctuations persist. Our bounds then give C eq = 2 ∥ Q ∥ L ∞ ( µ ) and D eq = 0, rendering fluctuations purely sub-Gaussian, exp[ - ta 2 / (2 C eq )], at all deviation scales. Out of equilibrium ( j s = 0), dissipation enters quantitatively , widening the Gaussian scale by a factor of (1 + Σ U /λ gap ), and a qualitatively new , exponential scale emerges which, beyond a crossover of a ∗ ≡ C / D , dominates and leads to heavier-than-Gaussian tails for large deviations. Broken detailed balance thus leaves a fingerprint on the shape of the fluctuations of any current.

Conclusion.- Leveraging the Cram´ er-Chernoff method we derived nonasymptotic bounds on the probability that an observable generalized current J t , or its sample mean over any arbitrary sample size n , of a geometrically ergodic diffusion deviates from the stationary mean E µ [ J t ] by more than any amount a . The concentration is governed by the relaxation time of the diffusive dynamics, the locally observed dissipation rate, and the intrinsic local fluctuations of the observable, and reveals qualitative and quantitative differences in fluctuations in and out of thermodynamic equilibrium. We further obtained refined inverse thermodynamic uncertainty relations, bounding generalized current variances from above, and constructed nonasymptotic confidence intervals enabling uncertainty control in thermodynamic inference from short trajectories and small samples. Importantly, Eqs. (16) and (17) provide the first quantitative answer to when a trajectory is sufficiently long and a sample is sufficiently large. We applied our results to currents observed on a two-dimensional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process in and out of equilibrium and rigorously detected broken detailed balance. Further practical applications and results for sub-geometrically ergodic and underdamped (phase-space) and multiplicative noise [109] diffusions, and Markov-jump processes, will be addressed in future works.

The crucial implication of all results is not that centrallimit approximations can become inaccurate; unlike our bounds they can be violated (see Fig. 2b,d), and by significantly underestimating tail probabilities, confidence guarantees based on these arguments have less coverage than required and fail to be valid.
