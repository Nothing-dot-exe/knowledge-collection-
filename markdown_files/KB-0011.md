## AI-Assisted Design of a Post-Quantum Cryptographic Accelerator: A Deployed-Silicon Case Study

Jungmin Park, Eunha Kim, Wooseop Kim, Seongjoon Cho, and Byungho Cha

Abstract -Post-quantum migration is mandated on published timelines, and silicon that ships with a defect cannot be patched remotely. The standard acceptance gate cannot detect an entire class of ML-DSA defects. Signing resamples until a candidate meets its norm bounds, so the executed path varies with the message, whereas known-answer tests (KATs) sample fixed values and reach only the depths their seeds trigger. Our accelerator passed its full KAT regression while carrying a norm check that outran block-RAM latency, leaving each candidate's final coefficients unverified; the escape surfaced at reject-loop iteration 5. The blind spot lies in the instrument, not the engineer; care cannot remove it. We replace that gate. A byte-exact golden-reference oracle paired with randomized adversarial soak drives the rejection loop past any fixed vector, closing the gap: 301,343 data-dependent signings, zero escapes. Because the gate judges artifacts and never authors, trust becomes separable from authorship, making AI authorship an answerable question. We report 232 logged experiments in which an agentic large language model drove a unified ML-KEM-768 and ML-DSA-65 accelerator with on-chip key custody from RTL to PCIe bring-up on one Kintex-7 XC7K160T, shipped at 98.5% slice occupancy. Success was 71.6%, following a hardware-coupling gradient, 77-85% for documentation and research against 50-53% for synthesis and bring-up, which observability can explain: failure concentrates where corrective signals are physical-side only. That so unreliable an author produced an artifact byte-exact across all six FIPS operations-its deployed baseline surviving the same 779,945check zero-failure soak-is the claim.

## I. INTRODUCTION

Post-quantum cryptographic hardware is essential yet errorprone, and the test that decides whether it may ship is blind to an entire class of its defects. NIST closed PQC standardization in August 2024 with FIPS 203 (ML-KEM [1], [2]) and FIPS 204 (ML-DSA [3], [4]). CNSA 2.0 [5] and NIST IR 8547 [6] attach dates, the latter proposing deprecation of 112-bit-security RSA and ECC by 2030 and disallowance of all quantum-vulnerable algorithms by 2035. Migration is therefore a scheduled compliance obligation, and it lands hardest on hardware. Roots of trust, secure elements, and signing devices stay in the field for a decade or more and cannot be patched remotely. A defect shipped in silicon is a recall, not a release.

The defect class that concerns us is structural: ML-DSA signing is rejection-sampled. The signer resamples until the candidate satisfies its norm bounds, so the number of iterations, and with it the control path exercised, depends on the message. Correctness is therefore a property of execution paths . Known-answer tests sample values instead, and a fixed vector set reaches only the reject-loop depths its own seeds happen to trigger. Enlarging that set does not help: the coverage limit follows from how the vectors are built, not from how many there are.

Our accelerator passed its full KAT regression while carrying BUG-IDE-037: the rejection-sampling norm check iterated over all 256 coefficients of each polynomial, but blockRAM read latency delivered the final one or two after the accept/reject decision (Section V-D). The defect was reachable only at reject-loop iteration 5, 1 for one message class, and every affected signature failed verification. It is one of eight defect classes we catalogue, each caught by a validation layer the others miss (Table II).

The gap lives in the instrument: a careful team using KAT as its acceptance gate ships the same defect. We therefore replace the gate with a byte-exact golden-reference oracle paired with randomized adversarial soak that drives the rejection loop past any fixed vector's reach. Because the replacement judges artifacts and never authors, it is author-agnostic : who wrote the RTL becomes a separate question.

That separation is what makes the AI question worth asking. The deadline is fixed, engineers fluent in both lattice cryptography and FPGA timing closure are scarce, and agentic LLM coding assistants are already entering hardware flows. The open question is not whether they will be used on PQC silicon but where in the flow they can be trusted . The LLM-for-RTL literature evaluates snippets and single modules by pass@ k at simulation or lint [7], [8], [9], and even agentic, tool-inthe-loop flows [10], [11], [12], [13] stop short of validated, hardened operation on deployed silicon. No published study reports how assistant reliability varies across the phases of a real campaign.

Across the 2026 development cycle (2026-01 through 202606) an agentic LLM assistant drove design, verification, synthesis, host-software development, silicon bring-up, and security hardening of a unified ML-KEM-768 + ML-DSA65 accelerator with on-chip key custody, deployed on an XC7K160T Kintex-7 over PCIe Gen2 × 8. All 232 experiments were logged. 'Silicon' herein means the physically deployed FPGA in live operation, not simulation.

Three constraints made that campaign adversarial. Area was binding. Both NIST Level-3 schemes plus the custody HSM had to fit one mid-range XC7K160T, which forced a serialized number-theoretic transform (NTT) and still left the shipped build at 98.5% slice occupancy. The limit was placement density rather than arithmetic (Sections V-B and IV-F). Timing was tight. The 500 MHz domain of the Xilinx XDMA PCIe bridge sets the design's worst-case setup slack at +0 . 049 ns (0 failing among 178,247 endpoints design-wide); the path tipped negative during bring-up and was recovered by a seed sweep because the cause was congestion, not logic. Observability was the worst of the three. A stale command register survived softreset, wedging DMA ∼ 1-in-20 yet staying hidden for weeks because the soak harness self-healed it. That did not stop the assistant from reaching a confident, plausible, and incorrect diagnosis in ten logged episodes (Section IV-F).

Where the corrective signal for a task sits in an artifact the assistant can read, it performs at software-domain rates. Where that signal exists only on the physical side of the boundary, in DMA-engine residue, reset-domain asymmetry, placement density, or silicon-versus-simulator semantics, no reasoning over readable artifacts recovers it, and reliability falls to roughly a coin flip. This hardware-coupling gradient is a claim about observability , not that hardware is harder; its remedy is to make the physical side readable.

This work makes three contributions:

- (C1) A validation construction that closes a gap KAT cannot reach. Fixed-vector testing is structurally incomplete for rejection-sampled signature hardware; we demonstrate the consequence on deployed silicon (BUG-IDE-037, passed by a full KAT regression) and close it with the oracle-plus-soak gate above, over 301,343 data-dependent signings with zero escapes. The construction applies to any ML-DSA hardware effort; we generalize it into nine honesty constraints under which trust derives from validation artifacts, never from authorship (Section VIII).
- (C2) The hardware-coupling gradient and its cause. From 232 structured logs (40 task categories, 76 milestones) we show that LLM success declines as tasks approach FPGA placement, timing, and live-hardware state (7785% documentation and research; 50-53% synthesis and bring-up), and we identify the mechanism: failure concentrates where corrective information is unobservable in any artifact the model can read. We also report an eightclass bug taxonomy and ten 'confidently wrong, then corrected' episodes (Sections IV-E and IV-F).
- (C3) Silicon existence proof under binding constraints. The loop produced a unified ML-KEM-768 + ML-DSA65 accelerator plus key-custody HSM co-resident on one XC7K160T at 98.5% slice occupancy with 49 ps of setup margin at 500 MHz and hardware-RNG-seeded keys under a device-bound key-encryption key (KEK)-

a combination that is, to our knowledge, unique among unified PQC accelerators. The artifact is byte-exact across all six FIPS operations, survived a zero-failure 779,945check soak on the deployed firmware baseline v88 (v N labels successive firmware builds), and was productized through two releases to the final FIPS-203/204 wire formats (Sections V-VI). That an author measured at 71.6% task success produced an artifact with zero validation escapes is the concrete demonstration of (C1) .

Efficiency here means consolidation rather than speed: both schemes plus the key-custody hardware security module (HSM) share one runtime-modulus-switched NTT/INTT datapath and one Keccak-f[1600] core. Security means key custody: private keys are generated, wrapped, and used on-die, device-bound and adversarially validated. The claim is device binding, not at-rest secrecy and not side-channel resistance. Table V places the artifact against the closest unified MLKEM + ML-DSA accelerators on exactly that basis.

Two 2026 works combine LLMs with PQC hardware, LLM4PQC [14] and Liao et al. [15], the latter the first quantitative LLM-versus-HLS comparison (up to 2 . 6 × kernel speedup); the delta is one of depth rather than kind, contrasted axis by axis in Table VI (Section VII-D).

## II. BACKGROUND

## A. Preliminaries

ML-KEM-768 (FIPS 203) is a Module-LWE (MLWE) keyencapsulation mechanism with rank k = 3 over R q = Z q [ x ] / ( x n + 1) , n = 256 , q = 3329 , ζ = 17 . Hashing uses SHAKE128/256 and SHA3-256/512 (FIPS 202 [16]). Wire sizes are encapsulation key 1184 B, decapsulation key 2400 B, ciphertext 1088 B, and shared secret 32 B.

ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) is a Fiat-Shamir-with-aborts lattice signature (MLWE and Module-SIS problems) with n = 256 , q = 8380417 , ζ = 1753 , ( k, ℓ ) = (6 , 5) , SHAKE256 mask expansion, and rejection-sampling bounds γ 1 = 2 19 , γ 2 = ( q - 1) / 32 , β = 196 , ω = 55 . Wire sizes are public key 1952 B, secret key 4032 B, and signature 3309 B.

Shared-primitive opportunity. Both schemes share Z q [ x ] / ( x n + 1) , n = 256 : the NTT butterfly is identical in form across both moduli, and both rely on the same Keccak permutation, so resource sharing is natural and both schemes fit together on a Kintex-7 XC7K160T (Section V-B).

## B. LLM-Assisted Hardware Design: A Brief Primer

LLMs can generate syntactically valid Verilog [7], [8], [9], close simulation-feedback loops [10], [11], [12], [17], and assist verification and security-sensitive RTL repair [18], [19] (surveyed in Section VII). To our knowledge, no prior work closes the full path (RTL generation, synthesis, host software, live-hardware bring-up, iterative hardening) on a complete cryptographic system while reporting a campaignlevel longitudinal dataset of LLM-task outcomes.

## A. The Assistant and Its Toolchain

The design assistant is Claude Code, an agentic commandline interface giving the LLM file-system and shell tools and the ability to spawn specialist subagents. On wide-scope integration days an Opus-4.6 team-lead orchestrated multiple Sonnet-4.6 subagents. All models were Claude-family: Opus4.8 (93 logs, 17 with a 1M-context window), Opus-4.7 (63), Opus-4.6 (47), Opus-4.5 (24), Sonnet-4.6 (3, specialist subagents), and Fable-5 (1, deep research); counts are per-log model attributions (one log lacks a tier).

The assistant wrote Verilog-2001 RTL in rtl/ . It ran Icarus Verilog (iverilog) simulation with GTKWave review on failures, and Vivado 2024.2 batch synthesis and implementation via assistant-generated Tcl ( ExtraNetDelay\_high placement for congested respins, per-clock Intra-Clock-Table timing analysis). It maintained Python golden references for FIPS 203 and FIPS 204 (round-3 wire formats through v88, FIPS-final thereafter; Section VI-E), byte-compared against simulation and silicon. It drove Python ( pqc\_pcie\_host.py ) and C99 ( sw/c/ ) host libraries over XDMA PCIe DMA. A separate agent thread ran multi-agent adversarial review of critical design premises, catching at least one wrong optimization premise (EXP-20260604-001, Section IV-F).

## B. Context Architecture: The Persistent Markdown Stack

Prompts were typically one to three sentences plus pasted tool or hardware output; the harness injects a four-layer stack of persistent Markdown files into every session-three layers versioned in the repository, plus a typed memory in the harness's per-project store.

- Project constitution ( CLAUDE.md , ≈ 480 lines; P2). Repository layout, Verilog conventions, the XC7K160T resource budget, canonical tool commands, the logging template of Section III-C, and both schemes' mathematical parameters.
- Typed persistent memory (42 topic files at the 2026-0626 corpus snapshot, plus a one-line-per-entry index; P5). Each file records a single fact in YAML front matter, written unprompted by the assistant. The 21 project fi les hold live status; the 21 feedback fi les hold failure-distilled engineering rules with explicit why and how-to-apply fields-e.g., 'on a ≈ 86% -LUT, sub-0.1-ns-WNS design, even a no-op scaffolding port can break unrelated timing,' which later prevented repeat failures.
- Specialist-agent definitions (seven role files). Role prompts with restricted tool allowlists. The fan-outs of Section III-A instantiate these.
- Procedural skills (five skill files). Checklists invoked as named commands-e.g., /update-all and /spawn-team -making the logging discipline of Section III-C mechanical.

In our experience the feedback layer carries the most methodological value: a lesson learned in week n changes assistant behavior in week n + k (Section IV-F, lesson themes). Because the layers outlive any conversation, work resumes after total context loss without re-teaching.

One further persistent artifact sat outside the stack: a planand-milestone roadmap in the project dashboard's timeline data, decomposing the six-month effort into an ordered sequence (M1-M8bp, 76 entries; Fig. 2). Each milestone's 'done' is verifiable, so a resuming session inherits the last closed increment and the next target. M1-M8 were fixed through the v1.0 release; M8a-M8bp were appended afterward for the unanticipated silicon bring-up, key-custody, and productization work. We therefore read its role as purely structural and do not claim the plan raised the per-task success rate ( n =1 ; Section IV-G).

## E. Human-in-the-Loop Role

The developer (the first author) posed tasks, gated every generated artifact (no output entered the simulator, synthesis, or the board without review; ≈ 50% of edit-tracked generations needed a corrective edit, Section IV-D), supplied build reports and waveforms as corrective feedback, decided when to roll back, and made all real hardware decisions . The claim under test is that the agentic loop with these human gates - not unaided model authorship-carried the complete flow to deployment.

## F. A Reusable Protocol for Other Implementations

Applying the loop to another scheme, accelerator domain, or FPGA/ASIC flow takes six steps:

- P1. Executable golden reference first. Before any RTL, byte-exact agreement with the reference (here, Python FIPS 203/204) is the sole acceptance criterion for every artifact (Section III-D).
- P2. Write the constitution. One versioned Markdown file (Section III-B) of layout, conventions, budget, tool commands, and logging schema.
- P3. Instrument before generating. Adopt the experiment log (Section III-C) from day one.
- P4. Gate by hardware coupling. Every artifact crosses the review gate (Fig. 1); concentrate review depth where physical coupling is high (the 50-53% buckets of Table I), lighter-touch where low (77-85%). Here every artifact was gated (Section III-E); the coupling-success ordering is offered as a default prior, not a validated predictor.
- P5. Convert every root-caused failure into a typed feedback rule with why/how-to-apply fields (Section III-B).
- P6. Promote only up the ladder of Section III-D; never skip a rung.

The scheme-specific investment was the golden reference and KAT vectors. The device-specific investment was the constitution's tool-command and resource-budget sections. Everything else (the memory-stack schema , agent roles, logging schema, and promotion ladder) remained unchanged through the corpus-frozen v88 and the shipped v89. Whether it transfers with a similar success profile is untested (Section VIII, H1-H2).

## IV. EMPIRICAL RESULTS

## A. Corpus and Overall Success Rate

The 232-experiment corpus, frozen at the 2026-06-26 analysis snapshot, records one intensive campaign (2026-01 to 2026-06) spanning 40 task categories and 76 named milestones (M1-M8bp). 2 Of the 232, 166 score success and 66 failure, an overall success rate of 71.6% (Wilson 95% interval 65 - 77% ). Generated or modified code sums to ≈ 26 , 373 lines.

An experiment scores success when its log records the task's acceptance criterion met at close, after any in-experiment iteration and human edits: functional correctness where a functional oracle exists (byte-exact against the FIPS reference, a passing simulation, or on-silicon validation), the applicable pass field otherwise. Abandoned or rolled-back outcomes score failure. The 49.2% manual-edit rate of Section IV-D measures intervention en route . The syntax\_correct fi eld is not an independent gate: it is recorded (always True ) exactly in the 166 accepted logs and omitted in the 66 failures, so its count coinciding with the success count is definitional.

## F. Ten Episodes of 'Confidently Wrong, Then Corrected'

In ten episodes the LLM (or the developer acting on its output) reached a confident, incorrect conclusion, acted on it, and later corrected course-the hazard of plausible but wrong diagnosis. Five in detail:

(1) Premature 'zero-failures' claim (EXP-20260614001; corrected in EXP-20260615-001): a ≈ 84 , 000 -check soak prompted it; later soaks surfaced BUG-IDE-037, misdiagnosed twice until proven deterministic (Section V-D). (2) A 'driver flake' that was hardware (EXP-20260619-001): BUG-IDE-021, managed for months as such, was a stale HSM\_CMD level register surviving a soft-reset. (3) Draining the wrong FIFO (EXP-20260620-002): a stuck C2H (card-to-host) path drew two wrong 'drain the FIFO' remedies; ≈ 1041 B sat in the C2H engine , not the char FIFOfixed by a burn-op ( \_warmup\_c2h ). (4) 'LUT-bound' that was placement density (EXP-20260604-001): a multiagent adversarial review overturned the 'LUT-bound / idle DSPs' premise; the actual constraint was 97.49% slice occupancy. (5) An 'occasional' bug that was deterministic (EXP-20260529-001): Dilithium Verify failures called 'occasional contamination' were 200/200 deterministically wrong (BUG-IDE-036, Table II). ## A. Unified Architecture

As Figure 3 shows, the host communicates over AXI4Lite (BAR0 control/status registers) and AXI4-Stream (hostto-card/card-to-host, H2C/C2H, bulk data) via Xilinx XDMA IP. Clock domains: 100 MHz core ( pqc\_top\_clk ), 250 MHz XDMA AXI ( axi\_aclk ), 500 MHz PCIe transceiver ( userclk1 ) (Section VI-A).

Shared NTT/INTT datapath. A single 5-stage pipelined butterfly (5-cycle latency, one butterfly per cycle after fill) iterates all 7 (ML-KEM) or 8 (ML-DSA) NTT layers; twiddle ROMs hold both moduli's tables with the active modulus selected per transform, and the twiddle multiply is reduced by a two-cycle Montgomery [20] step. NTT-domain multiplication instead uses Barrett reduction [21]- m = 5039 , k = 24 for ML-KEM ( q = 3329 ) and m = 8396807 , k = 46 for ML-DSA ( q = 8380417 )-keeping coefficients in the normal domain: a degree-one base multiply for ML-KEM's incomplete NTT, a pointwise multiply for ML-DSA's complete NTT.

Shared Keccak core. A single Keccak-f[1600] unit serves both control FSMs and the key-custody keywrap/deterministic random-bit generator (DRBG) through a registered two-master mux, whose one extra command-bus cycle, invisible to directwired unit testbenches, caused a wrong-tag failure in an intermediate custody-era build (v79c), caught only on hardware.

On-die entropy (HRNG). A hardware RNG ( qec\_aggregator ) supplies all internal randomness. Four off-die Am-241 quantum-entropy chip sites (QEC [22]; one selected at runtime via HRNG\_CTRL[8:7] ), sampled as pulse-arrival times, are XOR-combined with an on-die ring-oscillator TRNG, then conditioned by a SHA3-256 core private to the subsystem. The conditioned 256-bit words seed a SHAKE256-DRBG that has no Keccak core of its own: it time-shares the single PQC Keccak through a bypass mux under hardware mutual exclusion-the same consolidation reflex that dropped a redundant ≈ 1 . 5 k-LUT Keccak during timing closure. Per-channel SP 800-90B health tests (repetition-count, adaptive-proportion) report through HRNG\_STATUS but are advisory in the deployed build; a sparse Am-241 pulse train trips them on a healthy chip. With HRNG\_CTRL[9] set, ML-KEM and ML-DSA KeyGen draw their 32-byte seed from the DRBG (the internal-seed path, Section VI-E), so the seed never crosses PCIe-unlike prior PQC accelerators, which receive seeds or keys from the host (Section VII). Entropy-validation status: H7 (Section VIII).

## D. BUG-IDE-037: The Methodology's Key Catch

Detection and symptom. A later seed-driven randomized mixed-operation soak ( ≈ 2 , 200 -vector pool) caught a verification-failing signature for one message class (EXP20260615-001); the preceding ≈ 84 , 000 -check soak (EXP20260614-001) passed clean, prompting episode 1 of Section IV-F; a constant SHA-256 hash across reruns proved the defect deterministic, not transient or thermal.

Root cause. In dilithium\_sign.v , the rejectionsampling norm check reads coefficients from the main BRAM with 2-cycle latency, but the ∥ z ∥ , ∥ r 0 ∥ , and ∥ c · t 0 ∥ scan counts covered exactly the polynomial, so the last one or two coefficients arrived after the accept/reject decision. The failing vector (vec76, reject-loop iteration 5) hit this blind spot: its sole norm-violating coefficient, r 0 [ poly [5]][ coeff [0]] , was last in scan order; a spec-correct signer rejects the candidate and first accepts at iteration 8.

Fix and validation. The fix extends each scan by the BRAM latency margin: z 1279 → 1281 (+2, wrapping), r 0 1535 → 1537 (+2, wrapping), and c · t 0 1535 → 1536 (+1, linear, plus a garbage-tail guard). On hardware the failing vector re-tested 20/20 byte-exact and the 7/7 KAT regression passed.

Detectability. Every affected signature fails verification (leakage caveats: H8, Section VIII). KAT vectors cannot trigger it: they never sample the message class that drives the rejection loop to the blind-spot iteration.

Reliability closure. An 8-hour soak ( c\_exhaustive\_8h\_soak\_hw ) ran 28,801.6 s, completing 779,945 checks with zero failures across ML-DSA provision (72,625), sign-from-vault → Verify on data-dependent messages (301,343), Verify tamper-reject (37,738), ML-KEM encaps → decaps-from-vault (295,673), and zeroize durability (72,562); ML-KEM provision (4) is thin coverage, a stated caveat. A separate C-library soak added 186,432 checks, also with zero failures.

## B. Host-Device Protocol

Control uses a compact bank of BAR0 registers ( CTRL , STATUS , SOFT\_RESET , the HRNG\_* entropy and HSM\_* custody banks, and Device-DNA readback; full map in the released manual). Dispatch is deliberately minimal: the host writes a 3-bit opcode to CTRL , streams input over H2C, and reads the fixed-length result over C2H, with no framing headers or length fields ( tlast delimits); the C2H read returning its expected byte count is the completion signal, since STATUS.done is a one-cycle pulse no PCIe-latency poll can catch (interrupts are unused), and STATUS.busy is busy-polled only as the re-arm gate. Wire sizes (v89 FIPSfinal, H2C → C2H, bytes): ML-KEM KeyGen 32 → 1184, Encaps 1216 → 1120, Decaps 3488 → 32; ML-DSA KeyGen 32 → 1952, Sign 4064 → 3309, Verify 5293 → 0. In internalseed mode ( HRNG\_CTRL[9] ) KeyGen seed bytes drop to zero and custody provisioning returns a wrapped blob (nonce + tag + ciphertext: 4096 B for ML-DSA-65, 2464 B for MLKEM-768). The direct Sign/Decaps formats carry the plaintext secret key over H2C (the compute/migration path); custody sign/decaps-from-vault instead re-presents the host-held, ondie-wrapped blob over BAR0 vault writes on each call and unwraps it on-die (the blob-transfer term of Table IV). The one exception to C2H result readback, ML-DSA Verify, reports its result in STATUS[2] dil\_valid , a late discovery.

The protocol also carries five operational disciplines , none in any component datasheet-each a distilled, logged silicon failure, protocol step P5 made visible:

- D1. Soft-reset + DVF\_STATE idle poll before every Verify ( DVF\_STATE taps the Verify FSM's live state over BAR0): else the second back-to-back Verify returns invalid deterministically (200/200; BUG-IDE-036, Section IV-F).
- D2. Inter-operation settle : consecutive ML-DSA operations need ≥ 1 ms separation. The fast path uses a busy-poll re-arm gate plus an unconditional 0.5 ms residue drain.
- D3. Warm-up burn-op at open : a ≈ 1 kB tail in the XDMA C2H engine survives close() → open() and soft-reset, shifting the next process's first result by one byte; one throwaway KeyGen flushes it.
- D4. FLR as transport recovery : a wedged H2C path, unreachable by any drain or soft-reset, recovers by scripted PCIe FLR in ≈ 6 s, no power-cycle.
- D5. Single-opener serialization : the XDMA device admits one opener ( EBUSY ). Multi-client use goes through the pqcd daemon (Section VI-D).

## C. The Bring-Up Record: Where the 50% Bucket Was Earned

The PCIe bring-up compressed into four weeks (bitstreams v11-v58b): first Gen2 × 8 builds on 2026-05-04 (v11), scripted flash on 05-17 (v13), first end-to-end PCIe operation (MLKEM KeyGen, v18) and first byte-exact result (v22) on 05-21 after fixing X-initialized $readmemh ROMs and the Vivado multi-write BRAM dissolution, and all six operations FSMcomplete on 05-22 (v29). Byte-exactness climbed 4/6, 5/6, 6/6 across 05-26/-27/-28 (the last a host-side fix, no reflash) and reached 6/6 at full back-to-back rate on 05-29 (mitigated host-side; D1).

Three failure families dominate the record (Table II):

- (a) Stream-handshake races in RTL. Combinational request lines into register-derived readiness, one-cycle pulses against back-pressure, target-switch first-byte duplication, and

compute-gap over-fetch all passed iverilog and failed on silicon; each fix was a registered request, a sticky flag, or a one-deep skid buffer.

(b) Host-vs-RTL misdiagnosis. The costliest family, and the observability problem in its purest form: host, driver, DMA, and RTL produce identical boundary symptoms, so byte-exactness locates a failure without attributing it. Three 'RTL bugs' were host code: an abandoned reader thread silently consuming device bytes (BUG-IDE-031); a C2H drain that silently no-ops off the main thread (BUG-IDE-033); and BUG-IDE-034, the host waiting for Verify result bytes the RTL never emits on C2H-masking two RTL repair attempts (v49, v51) until the register-level read was found on 05-27, its resolution exposing a further host-side tr -encoding mismatch (BUG-IDE-035). The inverse occurred once: the back-to-back defect (BUG-IDE-036, D1) presented as a driver flake but is RTL-behavioral. Attribution came only from differential A/B on live hardware (direct vs. threaded, C vs. Python, before vs. after FLR), distilled into standing rules: a failure that follows the host-side variable indicts the host, and no RTL hunt opens before a clean re-run. An X-clean full-module simulation, cheap via the golden-reference spine (Section III-D), exonerated the single-operation Verify datapath as correct since v51.

(c) Transport-layer defects. XDMA C2H shortcompletions, AXI-Stream padding misreads, and a ≈ 1-in-20 mixed-operation H2C wedge ( errno 512 , FLR-only) round out the family; the wedge was self-healed by the soak harness for weeks until root-caused (BUG-IDE-021). Its fix took a 500-iteration A/B from 24 wedges to one, then zero across a 1,500-iteration confirmation, 12,825/12,825 byte-exact.

The methodology reading: almost nothing here is cryptographic-it is boundary engineering, exactly where Table I shows coin-flip reliability (Section IV-D).

## D. Host Software Stack

Four host surfaces ship with the board, all implementing the protocol and disciplines above. A Python library ( pqc\_pcie\_host.py ) covers all six operations plus custody, D3 warm-up by default. A dependency-free C99 library ( libpqchsm ) is protocol-exact against it and is the Table IV bench client. The pqcd daemon serializes clients over the single-opener device (D5) and feeds a reference OpenSSL-3 provider that signed a TLS-1.3 CertificateVerify through hardware custody. The demo/validation suites add the check\_all\_six byte-exact gate and the self-healing soak harness of Section V-D. The redundancy is deliberate: C/Python divergences repeatedly localized transport bugs neither stack could attribute alone (Section IV-E).

## E. Productization and Bitstream Lineage Beyond the Snapshot

Release v1.0 (first cut 2026-06-21 on bitstream v87) packaged the bitstream, host libraries, daemon, suites, and manual. Release v1.1 (2026-07-08) ships bitstream v89, converting both schemes from round-3-derived encodings to the final published standards: FIPS-203/204 key-generation seed derivations, 4-bit SimpleBitPack w 1 encoding, a 48byte challenge, a signature grown from 3293 to 3309 bytes (the only wire-visible length change), and FIPS-204 domainseparated message hashing. With v89, device signatures are byte-identical to the FIPS-204 wire format, validated against the dilithium-py oracle, so conforming external verifiers (e.g., liboqs [29], OpenSSL) accept them with no host-side converter, and the device verifies external signatures directly. Release v1.1 was re-validated on the board: full suite set and adversarial soak, zero failures on v89.

Every corpus-derived Section IV statistic is quoted at the frozen 2026-06-26 snapshot (v88); the one flagged exception is Section IV-H's measurement window (six experiments beyond the freeze). ## VII. RELATED WORK

## A. LLMs for RTL Generation and Hardware Benchmarking

Thakur et al. [7] introduced RTL-generation benchmarks, VerilogEval [8] formalized pass@ k evaluation, and RTLCoder [9] contributed an open fine-tuned model and training set; ChipNeMo [38] domain-adapts models, and Chip-Chat [39] carried an LLM-co-designed microcontroller through a SkyWater 130 nm shuttle tapeout. The artifacts are snippets, modules, or that shuttle core validated at simulation, lint, bench demo, or expert rating; none reaches hardened, inservice silicon with a longitudinal outcome record.

## B. Agentic and Feedback-Driven LLM Hardware Flows

A second line closes a tool-in-the-loop cycle: AutoChip [10] iterates on testbench feedback, RTLFixer [11] repairs syntax, MEIC [17] and VerilogCoder [12] add functional debugging and planning, AssertLLM [18] generates assertions, and ChatEDA [13] orchestrates the EDA toolchain on benchmark designs (survey: [40]). None reaches validated operation on deployed silicon. QiMeng [41] spans RISC-V CPU spec-tosilicon but stops at bring-up of a demonstrator taped out by its pre-LLM pipeline.

## C. LLMs for Hardware Security

Ahmad et al. [19] use LLMs to fix security bugs in existing RTL rather than synthesize cryptographic hardware (survey: [42]); to our knowledge, no published work carries LLM-driven design with a cryptographic end-goal to validated operation on real silicon.

## E. ML-KEM/ML-DSA Hardware Accelerators

FPGA acceleration of CRYSTALS-Kyber (ML-KEM, FIPS 203 [1], [2]) spans a Pareto front from Xing and Li's compact 2-DSP/3-BRAM Artix-7 design [43] to the pipelined HPKA [44]; high-speed ML-DSA designs use parallel butterflies and multiple Keccak cores ( 2 × 2 NTT [45]); area-leaner designs cover all round-3 parameter sets [46], with compact variants at the far end of the area axis [47], [48].

Most directly comparable are unified Kyber+Dilithium designs: KaLi [23] (first, ZCU102, 270 MHz), Dobias et al. [24] (most resource-efficient to date, 17,138 LUT at 375 MHz), KiD [25], Wang et al. [26], Carril et al. [49] (HLS batch acceleration on a PCIe datacenter Alveo card), Beckwith et al. [50], and Truong et al. [51] (both FIPS-final standards, all phases, Zynq UltraScale+). Ours is (1) the only one, to our knowledge, on a mid-range Kintex-7; (2) integrated with on-chip key custody and HRNG-seeded on-die key generation for both schemes (prior unified designs receive seeds and keys over the host bus); and (3) measured end-to-end over PCIe including host-driver and custody overhead, not at the accelerator boundary. PUF-Dilithium [52] also targets on-chip key custody but stops at PUF-based seed protection of ARM software. A complementary line masks datapaths (among unified designs, Beckwith et al. [50] report SCA protection, and Dobias et al. [53] a first-order-masked, TVLA-evaluated datapath); this work deliberately makes no side-channel claim (Section VIII, H6).

Commercial PQC silicon. Table VII shows dedicated PQC silicon arriving in 2025-mid-2026. Caliptra's opensource Adams Bridge accelerator unifies ML-DSA-87 and ML-KEM-1024 as licensable datacenter-SoC IP [35], the same consolidation this design applies. At HSM scale, most shipping products run PQC as firmware on general-purpose secure processors [36]; the closest analogue is Entrust's nShield 5, a classical HSM field-upgraded with internal-FPGA PQC acceleration [37]. To our knowledge, no commercial product yet documents full hardware datapaths for both an ML-KEM and an ML-DSA parameter set with on-die key custody at host-attached (PCIe) HSM scale; that is this artifact's slot.

## VIII. THREATS TO VALIDITY AND LIMITATIONS

We state nine honesty constraints-not hedges but integral to the scientific claim.

- (H1) n =1 case study / self-reported logs. No human-only baseline was run: the productivity and cost figures of Section IV-I are absolute, not comparative. Labels, lesson coding, and the per-log edit/iteration fields are self-recorded, incompletely covered (179 of 232; bounds in Section IV-D), and not independently adjudicated; a different segmentation would shift the percentages, not the coupling-gradient ordering. Acceptance stringency also differs by bucket (operator judgment at low coupling, byte-exact at high; Section IV-B), so part of the gradient reflects adjudication. Corpus completeness rests on contemporaneous logging (Section III-C); abandoned work scores as failure (Section IV-A).
- (H2) Single LLM family / single operator. All sessions used Claude models and one developer; within this tightly coupled dyad, contributions are not separable, and generalizability to other LLMs, toolchains, operators, or task mixes is unknown-the bucket populations of Table I reflect this project's phase structure.
- (H3) Trustworthiness from validation, not authorship. Each RTL module is trusted because it passed byteexact golden-reference comparison and, on the deployed v88 baseline, the 779,945-check adversarial soak; the v89 release re-passed the full ladder, soak included (Section VI-E).

- (H4) Device binding, not at-rest key secrecy. Given 7-series bitstream-encryption weaknesses [54], the bitstreamembedded KEK SALT cannot be assumed secret against a physical adversary; a hardened key store is future work (Section IX).
- (H5) Integrated-system measurements, not a throughput record. Reported ops/s are measured at the peak fastpath level (the default correctness-first path adds ≈ 1 ms of inter-operation reset and drain), and the ML-DSA Sign figure reflects the benchmark's fixed low-rejection message (Section V-C).
- (H6) No side-channel resistance claims. No masking is applied to the NTT, Keccak, or sampling datapaths (future work: Section IX).
- (H7) Not FIPS 140-3 validated. The module is FIPS 203/204 byte-exact against the reference implementations (Section VI-E) but has undergone neither Cryptographic Algorithm Validation Program (CAVP) testing nor FIPS 140-3 validation; likewise, while the QEC source's min-entropy is characterized per SP 800-90B methodology [22], the deployed HRNG chain has no formal entropy-source validation-statistical batteries validate the conditioner output only.
- (H8) BUG-IDE-037 is fail-detectable, not a forgery vulnerability. The defect produces signatures that fail verification (fixed in v84); the rare pre-fix emissions are treated as a potential leakage surface, with no claim of security-neutrality.
- (H9) Single-operation-at-a-time / XDMA single-opener. ML-KEM and ML-DSA share the NTT and Keccak cores, so one operation executes at a time; the XDMA device is single-opener (D5).

## IX. CONCLUSION

The standard acceptance test for lattice-signature hardware cannot detect an entire class of defects: ML-DSA correctness is a property of control paths while known-answer tests sample values; BUG-IDE-037 passed a full KAT regression on deployed silicon. We resolved that blindness by replacing the gate rather than the author: acceptance decided by a byte-exact golden-reference oracle plus randomized adversarial soak301,343 data-dependent signings among 779,945 checks with zero escapes on the deployed v88 baseline. Nothing in that construction depends on who wrote the RTL, which made the AI question answerable. Across 232 logged experiments an agentic LLM assistant drove RTL through hardening at 71.6% success, following a hardware-coupling gradient (7785% documentation/research vs. 50-53% synthesis and bringup). The mechanism is observability, not difficulty: reasoning over readable artifacts cannot recover a corrective signal that exists only on the physical side of the boundary. The slope of this n =1 study is a hypothesis with a mechanism attached, not a calibrated constant. Not in question is the artifact: a unified ML-KEM-768 and ML-DSA-65 accelerator with onchip key custody on one mid-range XC7K160T at 98.5% slice occupancy and 49 ps of margin at 500 MHz, byte-exact across all six FIPS operations (shipped v89, re-soaked, zero failures).

Future directions. (i) A controlled multi-operator, multimodel replication with baselines and independent outcome adjudication beyond this n =1 case; (ii) genuine at-rest keyencryption-key secrecy via an eFUSE-protected UltraScale+ (XCZU7EV) port, already first-silicon byte-exact across all six operations under the same methodology; (iii) side-channel hardening by threshold implementation, where LLM-assisted masked-gadget generation and RTL-level leakage repair [55] could lower its cost; (iv) higher-throughput multi-butterfly NTTs on larger devices, in deployments where the core rather than the PCIe round trip bounds throughput; (v) the deferred custody-protocol specification and analysis, plus FIPS 140-3 and CAVP validation for regulated deployments; and (vi) context condensation-distilled digests of the slowly changing corpus to cut the context cost center directly (Section IV-I), in measured tension with the stale-prose lesson of Section IV-F. All inherit the same discipline: state the rung of validation that earns the claim, and claim nothing above it.
