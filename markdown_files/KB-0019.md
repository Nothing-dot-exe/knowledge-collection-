## THE CONFORMALLY INVARIANT METRIC ON CLE 4 II: EXISTENCE OF GEODESICS

EMMANUEL KAMMERER, KONSTANTINOS KAVVADIAS, JASON MILLER, AND YI TIAN

Abstract. We continue our study of the conformal loop ensemble (CLE) with parameter κ = 4, the critical threshold at or below which the loops are simple and disjoint, touching neither each other nor the domain boundary. This paper is the second in a series of three establishing that the loops of a CLE 4 uniquely determine a conformally invariant, local, and geodesic metric such that the metric ball growth from the domain boundary coincides with the uniform exploration of Werner and Wu. In this second paper, we prove the existence of geodesics, showing that any geodesic between two loops is supported on the CLE 4 loops (off a set of Hausdorff dimension zero) and does not intersect the domain boundary. Along the way, we establish sharp quantitative estimates for the CLE 4 metric geometry, including exponential tail bounds for rectangle distances and multi-scale four-arm SLE 4 non-intersection bounds for metric balls.

## 1. Introduction

1.1. Overview. The conformal loop ensembles (CLE κ for κ ∈ (8 / 3 , 8)) are collections of random non-crossing loops within simply connected domains in C [She09, SW12]. They are conformally invariant, meaning their laws are preserved under conformal automorphisms of the domain. CLEs are the natural loop variants of the Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE) [Sch00]. SLEs and CLEs are conjectured (and in several cases rigorously shown) to describe the scaling limits of interfaces in critical statistical mechanics models on planar lattices [Smi01, LSW04, SS09, Smi10] as well as on random planar maps [She16, LSW24, GKMW18, KMSW19, GM21a, GM21c].

In the same way as for SLE, the value κ = 4 is critical for CLE. For κ ∈ (8 / 3 , 4], the loops are simple and disjoint, touching neither each other nor the domain boundary. On the other hand, for κ ∈ (4 , 8) the loops are self-touching and can touch each other and the domain boundary [RS05]. In this paper, we focus on the critical case κ = 4. For κ ∈ (4 , 8) it is therefore possible to define a metric on the set of loops of a CLE κ by considering the graph whose vertices are the loops and two distinct loops are adjacent if they intersect. This metric is conformally invariant since CLE κ is conformally invariant. This is not naively possible for κ = 4 since the loops of a CLE 4 are disjoint.

Nevertheless, in our first paper [KKMT26a], we constructed a conformally invariant metric on the loops of a CLE 4 by taking subsequential scaling limits as κ ↓ 4 of the renormalized graph metric on the CLE κ .

The program of constructing the CLE 4 metric was initiated by Werner and Wu in [WW13b], where the distance from any loop in CLE 4 to the domain boundary was defined via a Poissonian construction starting from the domain boundary. Equivalently, Werner and Wu in [WW13b] constructed metric balls from the domain boundary, where a loop of CLE 4 is discovered when it is contained in the metric ball. They referred to this exploration as the uniform exploration (cf. also [WW17, AS18]). This terminology arises because, as one grows the uniform exploration from the domain boundary, the CLE 4 loops are discovered in a Poissonian manner, with each new loop being rooted at a point chosen according to harmonic measure on the boundary of the unexplored region (seen from the target point of the exploration) just before the loop is discovered. It was not proved in [WW13b] that the uniform exploration corresponds to the metric ball growth from the boundary for a metric defined on the entire CLE 4 , nor was it shown that such a metric would be determined by the CLE 4 .

We proved in our first paper [KKMT26a] that the growth process of metric balls from the domain boundary for our subsequential limiting metric indeed has the law of the uniform exploration. The aim of this second paper is to establish the existence of geodesics (see Figure 1) and analyze their properties; these properties will play a crucial role in establishing the uniqueness of the metric in the third paper of the series [KKMT26b].

We note that a CLE 4 metric (which coincides with ours by the uniqueness proved in [KKMT26b]) was previously constructed by Sheffield, Watson, and Wu in unpublished work, following an approach completely distinct from ours. In contrast to their indirect definition of the metric, we showed in the first paper that the metric arises naturally as a subsequential scaling limit as κ ↓ 4 of the renormalized graph metric on the CLE κ . Furthermore, they did not investigate the existence of geodesics, which is not automatically guaranteed by the subsequential limit as κ ↓ 4: although a geodesic exists between any two loops for the graph metric on CLE κ for κ &gt; 4, one must rule out the possibility that the loops along this geodesic disappear in the limit as κ ↓ 4; this is a subtle question due to the topology with respect to which the limit is taken. The results of this paper actually hold for any metric on CLE 4 satisfying a certain set of natural axioms. This paper can therefore be read independently of the first one, and the results proved here can be applied to other approximation schemes for the CLE 4 metric.

- 1.2. Geodesics in random metric spaces. We provide a brief background on the study of geodesics in random metric spaces.

In many settings, the existence of geodesics is a direct consequence of the convergence (possibly along a subsequence) of geodesic metric spaces in the Gromov-Hausdorff or Gromov-HausdorffProkhorov topology. This is the case, for example, for the Brownian continuum random tree [Ald91], the Brownian sphere (for which tightness was established in [LG07]), and the scaling limits of random planar maps with large faces [LGM11]. Similarly, for the Liouville quantum gravity (LQG) metric, the existence of geodesics in any subsequential limit of Liouville first passage percolation is a direct byproduct of tightness [DFG + 20]. However, in our setting, since the subsequential limit is constructed with respect to a different topology, the existence of geodesics is not automatic and has to be proved directly.

More generally, the study of geodesics has become a central topic in the field of random geometry. In the Brownian sphere, detailed structural properties of geodesics and of geodesic trees directed toward typical points were established by Le Gall [LG10]. Beyond existence and uniqueness, a recurring phenomenon across planar random geometry is the confluence (or coalescence) of geodesics: geodesics starting from different locations merge before reaching their destination. Geodesic confluence has been established for LQG metrics [GM20a], and the geometric nature of LQG geodesics was shown to be distinct from that of SLE curves [MQ20]. Confluence and geodesic network structures also play a central role in the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) universality class, notably in the directed landscape [DOV22, Dau25]. Conformally covariant metrics and their geodesics have also been studied on CLE carpets [MT25] and non-simple CLE gaskets [MY25].

Furthermore, properties of geodesics were a crucial tool in establishing the uniqueness of the Brownian sphere [LG13, Mie13] and of stable carpets and gaskets [CMR25]. They were also useful in the proof of uniqueness of the LQG metric [GM21b]. In a similar way, the existence and structural properties of geodesics established in this paper will play a key role in proving the uniqueness of the CLE 4 metric in our next paper [KKMT26b].

1.5. Techniques. Our toolbox combines several distinct perspectives on CLE 4 : the connection between metric balls grown from the boundary and the uniform exploration, the couplings of CLE 4 and of SLE 4 with the Gaussian free field via level lines, and the Brownian loop soup construction of CLE 4 .

It is well known that, due to the Poissonian construction of the uniform exploration [WW13b] (see Section 2.8), the time at which the loop surrounding the origin is discovered by the uniform exploration is an exponential random variable. We show that, similarly, the distance between the top and bottom sides of a conformal rectangle (defined in Section 3) has an exponential upper tail. This exponential tail is a fundamental ingredient in our analysis, as it yields bounds on all moments of the distance across a rectangle. In turn, these estimates provide tight a priori control on the macroscopic geometry of the space, preventing the metric from degenerating or blowing up.

Another key tool is the asymptotic behavior of the probability that two metric balls do not intersect before hitting a small Euclidean ball; see Proposition 4.10. More precisely, we show that the probability that the metric balls grown from two loops remain disjoint up until each of them first hits a Euclidean ball of radius ε is at most ε 2+ o (1) as ε → 0. Geometrically, this event corresponds to a 'bottleneck' where two distinct explorations come microscopically close without having merged. The main idea in the proof of Proposition 4.10 is to translate this non-intersection event into a requirement for four macroscopic arms. This allows us to apply the four-arm exponents for bichordal SLE 4 across multiple scales, using the arm estimates obtained by Zhan [Zha20, Zha21].

To establish the existence of geodesics, we employ a multi-scale construction that relies crucially on this 2 + o (1) exponent bound from Proposition 4.10. To construct a geodesic between two given loops, we iteratively identify a sequence of intermediate loops that serve as nearly optimal 'waypoints'. At each step of the iteration, we refine the spatial scale and apply our non-intersection estimates to prove that this sequence of waypoints converges a.s. This procedure yields a continuous curve whose D -length equals the distance between the two loops, i.e., a geodesic.

- 2.4. Conformal loop ensembles. Now, we will briefly review CLE and refer to [SW12, MSW17, She09] for more details.

A conformal loop ensemble (CLE) in D is a random countable collection Γ of non-nested and non-crossing loops in D that possesses the following properties.

- (i) (Conformal invariance) For any Möbius transformation ϕ : D → D , the laws of Γ and ϕ (Γ) are the same. This makes it possible to define CLE on any simply connected domain U ⊊ C as the image of a CLE on D under a conformal mapping from D onto U . (When the loops can touch ∂ D , i.e., for κ ∈ (4 , 8), the image is defined via prime ends.)
- (ii) (Domain Markov property) For any simply connected subdomain V ⊂ D , we let V ⋆ be defined as in (1.1) (with Γ in place of Γ U ). Then, conditionally on the collection of loops of Γ not contained in V , the restrictions { ℒ ∈ Γ : ℒ ⊂ V j } for the connected components V j of V ⋆ are independent, and each has the law of a CLE in V j .

It was shown in [SW12, She09] that for every CLE there is a unique κ ∈ (8 / 3 , 8) for which its loops can be constructed via branching variants of SLE κ called exploration trees; the loops are then SLE κ -type loops. Conversely, for each κ ∈ (8 / 3 , 8) there is a unique CLE with SLE κ -type loops, and we denote it by CLE κ .

If κ ∈ (8 / 3 , 4], we have that CLE κ consists of disjoint simple loops which a.s. do not intersect the domain boundary. However, if κ ∈ (4 , 8), we have that the loops in the CLE κ are self-touching and can touch each other and the domain boundary. In the present work, we will focus on the κ = 4 case.

- 2.11. Background on Carathéodory convergence. We gather here some equivalent definitions of Carathéodory convergence. Let ( U n ) n ≥ 0 be a sequence of simply connected domains in C . Let U ⊂ C be a simply connected domain. Let z ∈ U . Let f be the unique conformal map from D to U with f (0) = z and f ′ (0) &gt; 0; when z ∈ U n , let f n be the analogous map from D to U n . We say that ( U n , z ) converges to ( U, z ) as n →∞ in the sense of Carathéodory if z ∈ U n for n large enough and f n converges to f uniformly on compact subsets of D . The convergence does not depend on the choice of z ∈ U , so we will often say that U n converges to U in the sense of Carathéodory.

Let us also recall the original definition of Carathéodory convergence.

Definition 2.6. Let U ⊂ C be a domain. Let z ∈ U . Let ( U n ) n ≥ 0 be a sequence of domains in C . The sequence ( U n ) n ≥ 0 converges in the sense of Carathéodory toward U relative to z if

- For each compact set K ⊂ U , we have K ⊂ U n for all n large enough;
- For each connected open set V ∋ z , if V ⊂ U n for infinitely many n ≥ 0, then V ⊂ U .

The above definition is more general since it does not assume that the domains are simply connected. The equivalence between the two definitions in the case of simply connected domains is the content of the well-known Carathéodory kernel theorem (see, e.g., [Pom92, Theorem 1.8]).

Furthermore, by the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, the convergence in the sense of Carathéodory implies the uniform convergence of f - 1 n toward f - 1 on every compact subset of U .
